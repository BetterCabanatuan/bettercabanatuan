# Get Free Check-ups, Basic Medicines and Vaccines — Cabanatuan City

The City Health Office gives Cabanatuan City residents free medical consultations, in person or by telemedicine, and hands out available medicines after the check-up. Vaccines for children up to 24 months and pregnant women, anti-rabies shots, TB treatment, family planning and basic dental care are also free. This page tells you what to bring, how long each visit takes and which City Health Center serves your barangay.

## Who can use these services

Cabanatuan City residents. The Citizen's Charter notes that access may be limited during a pandemic. Patients in quarantine or isolation are exempt from appearing in person. Telemedicine consultation is also available.

**What to bring**

| 1. Go to the triage area            | Temperature check, health declaration and initial screening                                                                                                                      | 5 minutes                                                         |

| 3. Receive your prescribed medicine | The nurse gives the available medicine and extra health instructions                                                                                                             | 5 minutes                                                         |

## Free medical consultation (telemedicine)

Office: City Health Office, through your barangay's designated City Health Center. Fee: {FEE_CHO_SERVICES}. Total time: {TIME_TELEMED_SIMPLE} for a simple case, {TIME_TELEMED_COMPLEX} for a complex case.

**What you need**

- A mobile phone, internet access and social media.

**Steps**

1. Contact the City Health Center assigned to your barangay (numbers below). The nurse interviews you and makes an initial assessment (10 minutes).
2. The physician assesses you, gives medical advice, and prescribes medication or issues a laboratory request if needed (5-10 minutes simple, 15-20 minutes complex).
3. The nurse gives extra health and follow-up instructions (5 minutes).

## Basic medicines

The charter lists medicine as the last step of a consultation: after the physician prescribes, the nurse "provides available medicine" free of charge. There is no separate pharmacy service in the charter. If a medicine is not in stock, the office cannot supply it; ask the nurse on duty what is available.
